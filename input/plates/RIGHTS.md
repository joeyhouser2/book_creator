# Period plates for the Black Prince — sources and rights

SHA-1 checked against Commons), `commons.json` (the file's Commons metadata as
fetched 2026-10-12) and `print-gray.jpg` (greyscale, auto-contrast, for the
black-and-white interior).

All four are tagged Public domain on Commons with `Copyrighted = False` and no
attribution required. The tag is Commons' judgement, not a legal opinion: the
holding library's own terms were not read, so see "Still to check".

## Library reuse terms (looked up 2026-10-13)

Commons tags all four Public domain, and US law (Bridgeman v. Corel) gives a
faithful photograph of a flat public-domain work no new copyright. The holding
libraries' own terms are a separate matter, and are unresolved:

- **BnF / Gallica (fr. 236, f. 221v; btv1b8100132n).** The official terms page
  returned 403 and its usual URL 404. From secondary sources of about 2013-14:
  non-commercial reuse is free with the source named; *commercial* reuse (selling
  products built from the content) needs a paid licence, and high-definition
  files are ordered from reutilisationcommerciale@bnf.fr. These may have been
  revised since. A French parliamentary question and press coverage dispute the
  legal basis of charging for public-domain reproductions ("copyfraud"), but BnF
  has not withdrawn the terms. **Before sale, read the current terms on
  gallica.bnf.fr or write to BnF.**
- **Besançon (ms 864, f. 172).** No licence or reuse page found. The 2002
  digitisation of the Froissart *Chroniques* (mss 864-865) is recorded as jointly
  owned by the City of Besançon and the University of Sheffield; the library's
  catalogue gives the citation form "BM Besançon Ms X f. Y" and asks that
  publication of unpublished documents be notified (that wording covers the
  originals, not the images). Commons carries no library terms. **Ask the library.**
- **Kortrijk (cod. 135, f. 95v).** The Flickr album (Bibliotheek Kortrijk) shows no
  licence or rights statement, only the site's general Terms. Commons' "Public
  domain" rests on the work's age. **Ask the library.**
- **Louvre ring.** Photograph by Jastrow (2006), who dedicates his photographs to
  the public domain; confirm on the Commons file page.

Practical position for a small print run: all four images are 14th-15th century
and tagged public domain, and the two Poitiers images and the Crécy miniature
are 2-D art. The risk is a library's contractual terms, not copyright. The two
that matter most are BnF (stated commercial licence) and Besançon (unknown).
Replace either with the Kortrijk miniature or drop it if the library objects.
